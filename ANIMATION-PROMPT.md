# Prompt: "A Day in the Garden" — the jardinero.com hero animation

Use this prompt to recreate the animated hero scene from scratch.

---

Build a self-contained HTML + CSS hero-section animation (no JavaScript, no libraries) of a stylized light-theme landscape that lives through a never-ending cycle of days. The hero contains centered marketing text (eyebrow chip, headline, lede, CTA buttons); the landscape animates around it and never touches it.

**Scene layers, back to front:** a pale sky gradient; a celestial layer (sun, moon, four stars); three stacked rolling-hill silhouettes in layered greens (one SVG stretched full-width via `preserveAspectRatio="none"`, ridge crests near the top of its box so plenty of green shows above the fold); grass tufts along every ridge; two large stylized corner plants; and two full-bleed color-wash overlays (warm peach "sunset", cool blue "dusk") layered above the hills but below the text. All scenery is `pointer-events:none` and `aria-hidden`. Z-order matters: celestial layer BELOW the hills (so rising and setting are true occlusion), plants and grass above the hills, tints above those, text above everything.

**Master clock:** one solar day = 40 seconds. Every animation is `40s linear infinite` (the meadow uses `120s`) and they all start together, so the whole scene is synchronized purely by keyframe percentages — pausing every animation at the same currentTime must always produce one coherent moment of the day. Animate only `transform`, `opacity`, and `stroke-dashoffset`.

**The sun (daytime, 0–62%):** a warm radial-gradient disc with two soft box-shadow glow halos, anchored to a horizon baseline derived from the hills' height (a shared CSS variable). It rises from behind the hills at the far left, climbs the text-free left margin, crosses the narrow empty band above the headline (apex dead-center at 31%), descends the right margin, and sinks behind the hills at 62%. While hidden below the horizon it slides back to the left at opacity 0 and breaks the horizon again at 98–100%, whose keyframe values exactly equal 0% so the loop is seamless.

**Nightfall and night:** the sunset tint's opacity peaks exactly as the sun crosses the horizon (~62%, and again at dawn ~98%); the dusk tint holds through 66–88%, tinting hills, grass, and plants but never the text. A pale glowing moon (smaller disc, white-blue radial gradient, crisp 1px rim) arcs the same arch path from 66–95%; the stars fade in at ~70% and out by ~96%.

**The plants (one per bottom corner, mirrored):** each is an SVG with a curved main stem, two branches, and five leaves. Stems and branches are strokes with `pathLength="1"`, `stroke-dasharray:1`, and `stroke-dashoffset` animating 1→0 in a stagger through sunrise (4–24%), so the plant literally draws itself upward as the sun climbs. Leaves are pointed-oval paths that scale-pop (with a small overshoot) from their attachment points — `transform-box:fill-box; transform-origin:50% 100%` — in sequence from lowest to the crown leaf, which opens exactly at noon. Heliotropism: every leaf carries a second rotation animation sweeping from leaning east (−16°) at sunrise, to upright at noon, to leaning west (+20°) as the sun descends — put rotation on the leaf path and the pop-scale on a wrapper group so the two transforms compose. The right plant is the same markup inside a `scaleX(-1)` container, with negated rotation keyframes so both plants face the same absolute direction. At dusk (56–67%) leaves fold in reverse order and stems retract, leaving bare ground at night so the plant can grow fresh at dawn.

**The meadow (three-day epoch, 120s):** ~170 grass tufts — each 2–3 short curved stroke blades with `vector-effect:non-scaling-stroke` — placed by sampling the actual cubic-Bézier ridge curves of all three hills (jittered spacing, height, and lean; taller and darker on nearer ridges). Split the tufts into three waves, each wave sub-staggered into three groups. The meadow's loop spans three solar days: wave one sprouts through day one's morning (scaleY 0→1 pops from the ridge line, origin at the roots), wave two joins at day two's sunrise, wave three at day three's — so every time the sun comes up, more grass grows, and the field visibly thickens day over day. In the deepest night of day three (~97–99% of the 120s), all grass fades out together so the epoch restarts invisibly.

**Responsive:** drive the hills' height (and the sun/moon horizon baseline) from one CSS variable — taller cap on large screens so green fills the lower third of the viewport. Under ~660px there is no text-free sky, so swap the sun/moon keyframes (via a media query that redefines the same @keyframes names) for a low roll skimming along the ridge line, hide the corner plants below ~1024px, and freeze the grass fully grown.

**Cursor interaction (the one piece of JavaScript):** on devices with a fine pointer, moving the cursor near the grass tramples it — every tuft within ~55px of the pointer fades out quickly (~150ms) and regrows about five seconds later (slower ~500ms fade back to whatever its day-cycle animation says it should be). Implement by caching each tuft's root position (bounding-rect bottom-center in page coordinates, recomputed on resize), listening for mousemove on the hero with a ~40ms time throttle, and toggling a `.trampled` class whose `opacity:0` is marked `!important` so it beats the running keyframe animations; keep one reset timer per tuft so re-hovering extends the bald patch.

**Accessibility:** every animation lives inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`. The base styles are a pleasant static mid-morning — sun up, plants fully grown and upright, full meadow, tints off — so reduced-motion users get the finished scene, not an empty field.
